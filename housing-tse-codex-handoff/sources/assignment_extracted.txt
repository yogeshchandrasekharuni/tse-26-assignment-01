TSE - Assignment  Metamorphic Testing of a Multi-Agent Housing-Trends Application Due· October 16, 2026;  Teams of 2 1. What you will build Build a small multi-agent application that analyzes residential housing listings in the Dallas, Texas metropolitan area and reports how prices have moved over the past twelve months. Several cooperating agents each scan listings against a set of user-specified criteria, and the system aggregates their findings into a one-year trend comparison. There must be a nice visual interface that users can interact with. The agentic workflow must not be a linear pipeline. Your design should include at least: (a) one feedback loop where a downstream agent sends work back upstream, (b) a conditional/iterative control step where an agent decides whether to stop or iterate based on a signal such as confidence or coverage, and (c) a genuine dependency where one agent's output changes what another agent does next, not merely what data it receives. Your design note (to be finalized and submitted in first 10 days) must show this control flow as a graph (with the cycles marked) and justify why the loops are necessary rather than decorative. In your final report, also include a short execution trace/log showing a loop actually firing at run time. Data source. Use any accessible source and document it: a public/edu real-estate dataset, a synthetic feed that you created, or a decent sized scraped/exported sample. You must ship a frozen local snapshot with your code so results are reproducible. Remember, live sites change, and your grade depends on re-runnable numbers !  2. Metamorphic testing Rather than checking each output against a known-correct value, you will define metamorphic relations (MRs). You transform an input, re-run the system, and check that the two outputs relate as the property demands. A violated relation exposes a fault without any oracle for the individual answer. Required: Design and empirically evaluate at least eight MRs spanning all three families (below). For each MR, state the relation, the input subdomain on which it should hold, the tolerance, and the fault it would expose if violated. The eight relations in the table are illustrative. You may adopt, modify, or replace them, but keep all three families. Before writing any MR, define your output contract: the exact set of statistics that make up “the 12-month trend” (e.g., per-month median $/sqft and active-listing count). Every MR is a statement about these specific outputs.  Example metamorphic relations (you may extend/modify these) Family Transformation Expected relation (the MR) Invariance Reorder / relabel the input listings; re-run The 12-month trend and all summary statistics are unchanged, order must not matter. Invariance Change currency display or units (USD↔USD-in-thousands; sqft↔same) Reported percentages and rankings are identical; only display scales. Invariance Duplicate every listing (2× the dataset) Medians and $/sqft trends are unchanged; only the active count doubles. Monotonic Add $X to every list price in one month That month’s median list price rises by ~$X; no month decreases. Monotonic Widen the price-band criterion (superset of listings) The active-count series is ≥ the original series, month by month. Monotonic Restrict to a sub-region (subset of ZIPs) Each monthly count ≤ the all-region count for the same month. Directional Shift the 12-month window forward by one month The 11 overlapping months’ statistics match the previous run’s overlap. Directional Scale all prices by a factor k > 1 Median $/sqft scales by ~k; the shape of the trend (% changes) is invariant.  Statistical checking. Because agents may be non-deterministic, check each MR statistically: run each relation over at least 30 distinct base inputs (criteria sets / slices), report the violation rate with a 95% confidence interval, and fix and record all seeds. The ≥30 minimum refers to distinct base inputs per MR, not total runs. For non-deterministic systems, repeat each

Getting-started hints • Keep agents small and deterministic where you can (temperature 0, fixed seeds). Non-determinism is exactly what your statistical MR checking is designed to handle. • Freeze the data first. Download once into a local CSV/JSON snapshot, then point every agent at the snapshot. This makes your MRs (reorder, duplicate, scale) trivial to apply and your results reproducible. • Write one MR end-to-end before building all eight as evidence of your harness (transform → re-run → compare with tolerance) on the simplest invariance (reorder listings) first. • Use a local model (Ollama + Llama/Qwen/Mistral) or free API tier for development. A small model is fine since you are testing the pipeline’s behaviour, not chasing benchmark quality.
